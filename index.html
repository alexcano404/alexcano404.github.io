Background

I was born in Medellin, Colombia, and later immigrated to the United States in 2014. After settling here, I pursued a dual major in Pure Mathematics and Economics at the University of South Florida. Under the mentorship of Dr. Thomas Bieske, I completed my undergraduate studies in 2022.

I am continuing my academic journey in the Mathematics Department at Purdue University. Under the guidance of Dr. Andrew Toms, my research centers on classifying normal operators in suitable classes of C*-algebras using tools such as the Cuntz semigroup.

Alongside pure mathematics, I work with Research Services in Purdue’s College of Agriculture and contribute computational support to the DARPA eX Vi project.

02 — Research

Operator algebras.

Classification · C*-algebras
Normal operators · Cuntz semigroup

Understanding the fine structure that distinguishes well-behaved algebras.

Elliott’s program to classify simple separable nuclear C*-algebras through K-theory and traces is essentially complete under the regularity assumptions of Z-stability and the Universal Coefficient Theorem. My aim is to move beyond the classifiable case and study the obstructions that arise for normal operators acting on separable Hilbert spaces in a unital C*-algebra of stable rank one.

This may require more delicate invariants, such as the Cuntz semigroup, or specialized tools from algebraic topology. I study the fine structures that help distinguish two well-behaved algebras.

01DARPA eX Vi↗

eX Virentia (eXVi) explores how existing, unmodified vegetation can act as a sensor for activities of Department of Defense interest by using natural plant responses to man-made chemical exposures.

The program brings plant biologists together with remote-sensing and modeling experts to study biochemical responses to chemical exposure and their externally observable characteristics. I provide computational and graph-theoretic support to Purdue’s College of Agriculture Research Services team.

Computational supportGraph theoryDARPA
02Rolls-Royce Data Mine↗

This project addresses the need of the aerospace propulsion system Prognostics & Health Management (PHM) team for synthetic data that mimics actual fleet data. The data can be shared with external research entities, including universities, to explore prognostic algorithms while respecting export and International Traffic in Arms Regulations (ITAR) restrictions on actual data.

I spent one semester organizing a student team and co-developing machine-learning models to support the Rolls-Royce PHM team.

Machine learningSynthetic dataPrognostics
03Two-layer neural network for MNIST↗

A basic two-layer neural network classifies handwritten MNIST digits without PyTorch or TensorFlow. It uses NumPy and Pandas, ReLU activations, softmax, backpropagation, and gradient descent. The original page reports accuracy above 91% after fewer than 500 iterations.

The implementation loads the MNIST CSV, normalizes grayscale values, trains a 784→10→10 network, evaluates on a development split, and plots sample predictions.

PythonNumPyPandasMNIST
04Wasserstein GAN↗

A PyTorch Wasserstein Generative Adversarial Network generates handwritten images from MNIST. The implementation includes a generator, critic, gradient penalty, training loop, saved generated samples, and training-loss curves.

PyTorchWGAN-GPMNIST
05Benamou–Brenier transport and FFJORD↗

This project solves a dynamic optimal-transport problem with a Neural ODE. A Gaussian distribution is transported toward a distribution sampled from an MNIST digit using a continuous normalizing flow, kinetic-energy regularization, and trajectory visualizations.

PyTorchNeural ODEOptimal transportFFJORD
05 — Student seminar

Student Operator Algebras.

Co-organized with
Hao Wan & Chrisil Ouseph
Wednesdays · 12:30

A student seminar to spark interest in operator algebras and build a community across graduate programs and research areas.

We introduce participants to topics in C*-algebra and von Neumann algebra theory, and to results, concepts, and tools that intersect with their own research and coursework. To give a talk, email cano13@purdue.edu.

Topics

  • Operator K-theory
  • Index theory
  • Topological dynamics
  • C*-algebra theory
  • von Neumann algebra theory
  • Noncommutative geometry and topology

References beyond Wikipedia

  • An Introduction to C*-Algebras and the Classification Program — Karen Strung
  • Von Neumann Algebras — Rolando de Santiago and Brent Nelson
  • An Introduction to K-Theory for C*-Algebras — Rørdam, Larsen, and Laustsen
  • An Introduction to C*-Algebras and Noncommutative Geometry — Heath Emerson
